How Netflix Uses AI to Recommend What You Watch

A one-page map of an everyday AI-powered system: the data it receives, how algorithms process that data, how recommendations are organized, and how the user's actions create new feedback.

Created for Grade 10 — AI Conceptual Understanding

The Big Idea

Netflix uses a personalized recommendation system to estimate which shows, movies, and games a member may enjoy. Netflix says the system uses signals from the member's interactions, information about titles, and patterns from members with similar tastes.

01

Data goes in

Viewing history, ratings, genres, title information, language preferences, device information, time of day, and how long a title was watched can provide signals.

02

Algorithms process it

Recommendation algorithms use these signals to estimate the likelihood that a member will enjoy different titles.

03

A personalized result comes out

The system chooses and ranks titles and can personalize which rows appear on the homepage and the order of titles within them.

Feedback loop: The user's new actions become additional signals. Netflix says it uses feedback such as titles started, titles completed, and ratings to continually update its algorithms.

What Happens at Each Stage?

1. Inputs: What data is collected?

  • Viewing history and interactions with Netflix
  • Ratings such as thumbs up or thumbs down
  • Genres and categories of titles
  • Title information such as actors and release year
  • Preferred languages
  • Device and time-of-day signals
  • How long the member watched a title
  • Patterns from members with similar tastes

2. Processing: What does the AI do?

The recommendation system processes these signals with algorithms. It estimates which titles a member may enjoy and uses the results to personalize the homepage.

Netflix describes several layers of personalization: the choice of row, which titles appear in a row, and the order of those titles.

3. Tools & steps: How is the output organized?

  1. Process available signals.
  2. Estimate likely interest in titles.
  3. Select relevant rows and titles.
  4. Rank the titles in each row.
  5. Display the personalized homepage.
  6. Collect new interaction signals.

4. Who uses the result?

The Netflix member uses the personalized homepage to discover something to watch. The system then receives new feedback from that interaction, allowing future recommendations to change.

Result: a personalized set of entertainment choices.

Example of the Feedback Loop

Imagine: A member often watches science-fiction shows and gives several of them positive ratings.

→ Data: Those interactions become signals.

→ Processing: Recommendation algorithms use the signals with other available information.

→ Output: The homepage can show and rank science-fiction titles or other titles predicted to be interesting.

→ New feedback: What the member watches next gives the system more information.

Proposed Improvement

Give users a “More Variety” control

A possible improvement would be to let users choose how much variety they want in their recommendations. This would allow a person to move between highly personalized suggestions and recommendations that are farther outside their usual viewing patterns.

More Like My History
More Variety

Predicted effect: More variety could help users discover different types of content instead of repeatedly receiving very similar recommendations.

Trade-off: Increasing variety may also produce recommendations that are less closely matched to a user's previous interests. The system would therefore need to balance personalization and discovery.

Why This Is an AI System

This system demonstrates how data, algorithms, tools, outputs, and users interact. The system does not simply store a list of favorite shows. It processes signals and makes predictions about what a member may want to watch. The user's future actions then provide new feedback, creating an ongoing cycle.

Netflix also publishes research on personalization, recommendations, machine learning, and search, showing that these areas are active parts of its technology research.
